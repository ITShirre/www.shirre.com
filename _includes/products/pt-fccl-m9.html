More data: M8-class reference and how it is measured

For reference: Dk 3.08 for M8 class (public vendor datasheet, 14 GHz). A line Dkeff and a laminate Dk are not strictly like-for-like.

How it is measured

  • Group delay: slope of the unwrapped SDD21 phase of the de-embedded differential line.
  • Length subtraction: delay(L330) − delay(L80) ÷ 250 mm (9.843 in); cross-checked with the 100 mm and 150 mm differences.
  • Speed and Dk: free space = 84.7 ps/inch, so v = 84.7 / 130 ≈ 0.65 c and Dkeff = (130 / 84.7)² ≈ 2.36.

Design impact

What it means for your design

M9-class loss in a thin, flexible mPTFE build, with a low and stable Dk.

Lower latency

Signals travel at ≈ 0.65 c, so long channels have shorter flight time.

Low, stable Dk

Wider traces hit the same impedance at the same thickness (lower conductor loss), or allow a thinner stack-up.

Tighter timing

More timing margin and lower intra-pair skew sensitivity for high-speed SerDes and 26.5 GHz+ links.

Consistent delay

Flat across frequency, so signals are predictable and low-dispersion.
