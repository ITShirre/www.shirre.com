PheroTetra® PT150 · benchmarked against M9 / M10-class CCL

PT150 stripline: M9-class loss in a 0.42 mm flexible build.

A 3-layer stripline built on 150 µm H-type PheroTetra® mPTFE FCCL. Measured on a 4-port VNA and de-embedded by length subtraction, it delivers insertion loss on par with M9-class rigid CCL, about 20% lower than M8 class, with a low, flat effective Dk across frequency.

Construction

PT150 3-layer stripline stack-up

The signal layer sits between two 150 µm PT150 (150 µm H-type PheroTetra®) dielectrics, bonded with PTNC16R film and shielded by top and bottom ground copper.

Layer build (top to bottom)

  1. 35 µmTop ground copper
  2. 150 µmPT150 mPTFE dielectric
  3. 25 µmPTNC16R bonding film
  4. 18 µmSignal copper (differential pair)
  5. 150 µmPT150 mPTFE dielectric
  6. 35 µmBottom ground copper
  7. 0.42 mmTotal thickness

How it is measured

  • Group delay: slope of the unwrapped SDD21 phase of the de-embedded differential line.
  • Length subtraction: delay(L330) − delay(L80) ÷ 250 mm (9.843 in); cross-checked with the 100 mm and 150 mm differences.
  • Speed and Dk: free space = 84.7 ps/inch, so v = 84.7 / 130 ≈ 0.65 c and Dkeff = (130 / 84.7)² ≈ 2.36.

Design impact

What it means for your design

M9-class loss in a thin, flexible mPTFE build, with a low and stable Dk.

Lower latency

Signals travel at ≈ 0.65 c, so long channels have shorter flight time.

Low, stable Dk

Wider traces hit the same impedance at the same thickness (lower conductor loss), or allow a thinner stack-up.

Tighter timing

More timing margin and lower intra-pair skew sensitivity for high-speed SerDes and 26.5 GHz+ links.

Consistent delay

Flat across frequency, so signals are predictable and low-dispersion.

Calculated, illustrative: a stripline with Dkeff 3.0 ≈ 147 ps/inch; 3.4 ≈ 156 ps/inch.
